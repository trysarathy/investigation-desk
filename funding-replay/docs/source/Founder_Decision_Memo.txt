When to fund a supplier payment
Recommendation: run a two-week replay experiment. Do not start a lender, switch rails or replace the
receipt product. Ask whether a Singapore importer can safely keep its own SGD usable for longer before
its US supplier needs spendable USD.
Reported in interview. An adviser describes an intermediary advancing money after credit review. This
establishes a plausible need for credit. It says nothing about how often a treasury team funds earlier
than its contract requires. The latter is the only problem this software can solve.
The test. Reconstruct one redacted week using only what the operator knew when the funding decision
was due. Compare its real policy, a spreadsheet with provider cutoffs, and a later funding slot. If the later
slot was not available or evidenced at the earlier decision, keep the baseline. Ask the controller what the
retained cash would actually have earned or repaid.
Synthetic economics. For USD 100,000 at SGD 1.35/USD, moving the commitment from five days to two
frees 405,000 SGD-days. At an assumed 8% differential that is SGD 88.77 gross; an extra SGD 10 of
processing leaves SGD 78.77. Idle cash with no incremental return makes the change SGD 10 worse. A
missed deadline can swamp either figure.
Buyer and offer. Start with the controller or CFO of a Singapore food importer paying US suppliers. Offer
one corridor, four weeks of read-only replay, a written exception report and a review for SGD 500,
subject to data permissions. Test SGD 270/month thereafter; neither price is validated.
Pass only with behaviour. Seek two unrelated firms sharing a permitted week of data, three defensible
discretionary decisions across them, and one signed paid scope. A hard deadline breach in a
recommended scenario stops that configuration. If contracts force early funding, redirect discovery to
the credit owner or the prior receipt-control hypothesis.
Tomorrow: request an introduction to one AP operator and one treasury signatory. Ask for the last case
before showing the simulation.
