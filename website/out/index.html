How it works

Every payment explains itself.

You create a payment request for each invoice in Clearline. From there, three moments, from your customer's screen to your finance team's.

Step 1 · Before anyone pays

Your customer sees exactly how to pay.

Each invoice gets its own payment page: the exact amount, the token, the network, and your receiving address split into groups that are easy to check.

  • Exact amount and network, no guesswork
  • The address comes from your own wallet; Clearline only reads it
  • In the demo, the page follows the payment from seen to confirmed to paid
Customer's payment page · demo data

Look-alike address check

A warning before the money moves.

Scammers plant addresses that start and end like yours in people's wallet history. If your customer pastes the address they're about to send to, Clearline tells them when it's a look-alike. Like your bank warning you before you pay a stranger.

Step 2 · When it's right

Correct payments tick themselves off.

Right amount, genuine USDC, right address, enough confirmations: the invoice is marked paid, with the reasons written down. Nobody has to chase it, and every decision can be reviewed.

  • Checks the token is the real one, from the issuer's own contract
  • Waits for confirmations before calling it paid
  • Every decision keeps its evidence
Finance view · demo data

Step 3 · When something's wrong

Problems explained in plain English, the reply already drafted.

Short, wrong network, counterfeit or unlabelled: Clearline says what happened and what's likely behind it, and drafts a polite reply for your customer. A counterfeit can't be marked paid by mistake.

  • Plain-English explanation, with the evidence underneath
  • Guesses are labelled as guesses
  • A reply you can copy, edit and send yourself

Built for

Finance and operations teams that get paid in stablecoins directly.

If your customers send USDC or USDT straight to your company's address (Web3 firms, agencies, service companies, exporters), nobody matches those payments for you. That's the job Clearline does.

Alongside your existing tools. Checkout pages and payment gateways usually handle payments made through them, and your wallet or custodian holds the funds. Clearline doesn't replace either. It reads the transfers that land directly with you, and explains them.

Trust and safety

Careful with money, by design.

Read-only

Clearline reads public blockchain records and your invoices. It never holds, moves or touches money, and never asks for wallet keys.

Shows its evidence

Every verdict lists what it checked: amount, token contract, address, confirmations. Inferences are labelled as inferences.

A person decides

It can block an obvious mistake, like counting a counterfeit as paid. Replies are drafts: you edit and send them yourself.

Your data, your call

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

What are stablecoins?+

Digital dollars. A stablecoin like USDC (issued by Circle) or USDT (issued by Tether) is meant to be worth one US dollar each, and moves between accounts on a blockchain in seconds, any day of the week.

Does Clearline hold my money?+

No. Clearline is read-only. It reads public blockchain records and your invoice list, and never holds, moves or has access to funds or wallet keys.

Which networks and tokens?+

Payment pages: USDC on Ethereum today. Checking a transfer someone reports: USDC and USDT on Ethereum, Tron and BNB Chain, including spotting payments sent on Polygon, Base, Arbitrum or Optimism instead. Design partners will shape what comes next.

Is it AI?+

The verdict (paid, short, counterfeit, wrong network) comes from fixed, written rules, and every verdict shows the evidence it used. An AI-written explanation is being built, but it hasn't been tested on real data, so nothing on this page relies on it.

How do my invoices get in?+

Today you create a payment request in Clearline for each invoice. Connecting your accounting software is something we'll shape with design partners.

Is it live?+

Not yet. It's a working demo running on fictional data. We're now looking for a small number of design partners to try it on their own payments.
