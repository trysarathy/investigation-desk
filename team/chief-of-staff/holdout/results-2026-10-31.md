## Gaps (general skill descriptions for the builder)
1. **Literal safety triggers.** When a kill-switch or stop rule's literal trigger seems to clash
   with what the founder probably meant, the employee reinterprets the rule. It should apply the
   safety rule as written, say why in one line, and give a one-step undo. A safety switch should never
   depend on the employee's reading of intent.
2. **Automatic safeguards treated as options.** It spots when an automatic protective threshold has
   been crossed. But it then offers "keep as is" as a choice, or even as the default. Once a
   threshold rule has fired, the compliant state is the starting point. Options should only be about
   what to do next.
3. **Staying inside the provided data.** It sometimes brings in figures from earlier context that
   aren't in the current data and labels them verified. Numbers in a run should come from sources
   opened in that run, or be labelled as not checked.
4. **Consistent metric definitions.** It sometimes picks different denominators for related rates
   in the same report, and one of them flatters the number. It should use one definition, state it,
   and stick to it.
5. **Third-party relationships and fallbacks.** When it holds a line against an outside
   stakeholder's request, it should also prepare a short, gracious note the founder can send. When a
   deadline is tight, it should include a ready fallback message.
6. **Middle-ground options.** In yes/no customer decisions, it should look for a workaround that
   meets the customer's underlying need without a risky commitment, and a quick clarifying question
   for the customer the founder could send.
7. **Human touch under pressure.** In reduced-time or stressful moments, it should add one short
   human line and keep the asks strictly to the stated maximum.
