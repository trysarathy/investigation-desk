# Cold-email craft for finance buyers (inside the GTM OS template)

Accessed 2026-10-29. The template is fixed by `gtm.lean.first_email` / `gtm.lean.followup` [VERIFIED]. Craft
lives in three slots only: **first line, question, close**. Everything else is generated by the code.

## 1. What the evidence says (directional, vendor data)
- Short wins: studies summarised by vendors put the best reply rates at roughly **50-125 words**, some under
  80 (Boomerang via YouGov https://yougov.com/articles/36339-keep-b2b-cold-emails-brief ; Belkins
  https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-email-benchmarks ; Instantly
  https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks) [ASSUMPTION: search-only, vendor sources].
  Our template body is ~110 words; keep the first line and question short so it stays there.
- B2B reply-rate benchmarks range from ~1.5% to ~7% depending on source and method [ASSUMPTION:
  search-only; vendor reports (instantly.ai, belkins.io) blocked 2026-10-31; even if opened they are vendor
  data, not neutral sources]. RULES.md's floor is 1% (throttle/downgrade); our working target is ≥ 5% on D0.
- The first follow-up adds a meaningful share of replies; later ones add little and raise complaints
  (Belkins) [ASSUMPTION: search-only]. That matches our D4 + D11 only.

## 2. How finance buyers read cold email [ASSUMPTION: practitioner judgement]
CFOs, controllers, finance managers and heads of accounts are sceptical of crypto hype and vendor email.
They answer questions about **their own process** from someone who has clearly read something specific
about **their** company. They care about month-end close, audit trail, exceptions, time spent and errors,
not "web3". So:
- Speak finance, not crypto: "invoice", "short payment", "reconcile", "month end", "books", "Xero".
- Ask about their process, never claim to know their pain.
- One idea per email. No feature list. No pricing. No "revolutionise".

## 3. The first line (≤ 18 words, true today, last 12 months, source URL logged)
Good sources, best first: the company's own payments/billing/invoicing page or FAQ naming USDC/USDT; a job
post for finance roles mentioning stablecoin reconciliation; their press release or blog; a named
interview. Weak: directories, aggregator lists, third-party "accepts crypto" sites (score.py marks these weak).

Rules:
1. Re-open the source in this run; the line must still be true on that page. Record `first_line_date` (the
   page's date, not today).
2. State the fact; don't flatter or interpret ("Saw your FAQ now lists USDT..." not "Love how innovative...").
3. Count words: ≤ 18. No numbers you didn't read. No names other than the recipient's company.
4. If the page is older than 12 months, or you can't open it → no first line → no send.

## 4. The one question
Make it answerable in two lines, about their workflow, tied to the first line. Bank:
| Evidence | Question |
|---|---|
| Accepts USDC/USDT on invoices | "When a USDC payment arrives short or without an invoice reference, who sorts it out, and how long does it take?" |
| Multiple networks (Tron + Ethereum) | "How often does a customer pay on the wrong network, and what happens next?" |
| Hiring for reconciliation | "How are stablecoin receipts matched to invoices today: by hand from the explorer, or with a tool?" |
| OTC / high volume | "How does your team confirm a USDT transfer is genuine and complete before marking an invoice paid?" |
Never ask the accounting question in D0: RULES.md keeps it for D4 and calls (D0 has one question only).

## 5. The close (goes after "...would help me a lot.")
The template already says "15 minutes of your experience would help me a lot." Don't repeat "15 minutes".
Use one of: "Would you be open to a short call next week?" / "Could we speak briefly one day next week?" /
"Is there a day next week that suits?" No links (the booking link only goes in replies to a "yes").

## 6. Worked examples (fictional companies from the eval fixtures)
Rendered with `lean.first_email`; personalised slots in **bold**.

> Hi Mei,
>
> **Saw your payments page now lists USDC for overseas freight invoices, updated September 2026.**
>
> I'm Aashmiin, a student at SMU in Singapore, building a small tool in this area (Clearline) and researching
> what happens when stablecoin payments arrive wrong: short after fees, on the wrong network, a fake token, or
> with no invoice reference.
>
> **When a USDC payment arrives short or without an invoice reference, who sorts it out today, and how long
> does it take?**
>
> I'm not asking you to buy anything. 15 minutes of your experience would help me a lot. **Would you be open
> to a short call next week?**
>
> Best regards,
> Aashmiin Kaur
> SMU student, Singapore · https://www.linkedin.com/in/aashmiin-kaur-479665391
>
> If you'd rather not hear from me, just reply "no" and I won't email again.

Subject is always "Quick question about stablecoin payments". D4 and D11 are generated verbatim by
`lean.followup` (D4 carries the accounting question); only the greeting changes.

**Bad (and why):** "Hi Mei, Clearline automatically reconciles your USDC and posts to Xero. Book a demo:
<link>" → pitches, over-promises ("automatically posts"), adds a link, no source, no opt-out.

## 7. Replies (in the person's thread, through the guard as step `REPLY`)
**Clear yes, BOOKING_LINK set** (auto): "Thank you, <name>. Here's my calendar, pick any 15 minutes that
suits: <BOOKING_LINK>. Aashmiin"
**Clear yes, no BOOKING_LINK** (draft + flag): propose 2-3 slots inside her free time and the prospect's
9:00-17:00, in **their** time zone with SGT in brackets. If they already named a time, confirm exactly that
time (see playbook "Meeting booking").
**Wrong person** (auto): "Thank you, <name>, that's helpful. Who on your team handles incoming payments? I'd
be grateful for a name. Aashmiin"
**Not interested** (auto): "Understood, thank you for letting me know, <name>. I won't email again. Aashmiin"
→ Do-not-contact.
**Not now / try later** (auto thank-you, no DNC): "Thank you, <name>, that makes sense. I'll leave it for
now. Aashmiin" → stop sequence, `next_action_date` at their stated time; re-contact later is a new first
email (approval rules apply).
**Pricing / product questions** (draft only): answer only with facts that are true today (see
`proposals-invoices.md`, COMPANY_RULES rule 2). Example draft for Aashmiin:
"Thanks, <name>. We're offering a small number of founding pilots: US$300 for 30 days, then US$200/month,
cancel any time. On Tron: today the pilot covers USDC/USDT on Ethereum; Tron isn't in scope yet, so I'd
rather be straight about that than over-promise. Would a 15-minute call help to see whether it fits?"
(Network scope must be re-checked against the product before sending.)
**Complaint** (draft only, DNC now): short, human, no argument, no legal citations: "I'm sorry, <name>. I
found your address on <source page> and wrote once to ask about stablecoin payments. I've removed you and
won't contact you or anyone at <company> again. Aashmiin"
**Out of office**: no reply; note the return date; push the next follow-up to the first business day after.
**Unsubscribe**: no reply. Do-not-contact the same day.
