## 1. The accounting: what is a USDC holding for an SGD-base company?

**Short answer: unsettled under IFRS / SFRS(I).** There are three positions. Each one leads to different month-end numbers.

| View | Basis | What happens at month-end in SGD |
|---|---|---|
| **Intangible asset, IAS 38** (cost model) | IFRIC June 2019 reasoning, applied by analogy | Not retranslated: stays at historical SGD cost. Impairment test only, so SGD strengthening gives a loss and SGD weakening gives no gain |
| **Intangible asset, IAS 38** (revaluation model) | Needs an active market | Revalued to fair value. Increases go to OCI, decreases to P&L |
| **Financial asset, IAS 32 / IFRS 9** (monetary item) | Holder has a contractual right to redeem at par | Retranslated at the closing rate (IAS 21). FX gain or loss goes to P&L both ways |
| **Cash equivalent, IAS 7** | Policy argument only | As for a financial asset, presented as cash. **Aashmiin rejected this for Clearline** (POSTING_RULES §9 decision 1) |

- **IFRIC June 2019 does not cover fiat-backed stablecoins on its own terms.** Its scope is a cryptocurrency that is "(b) not issued
  by a jurisdictional authority or other party" and "(c) does not give rise to a contract between the holder and another party". For
  that scope it concluded IAS 2 if held for sale in the ordinary course of business, otherwise IAS 38, and "not a financial asset",
  "not cash" [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/supporting-implementation/agenda-decisions/2019/holdings-of-cryptocurrencies-june-2019.pdf].
  USDC and USDT have an issuer and published redemption terms, so they fail (b), and arguably (c).
- **Why the FX answer depends on classification.** The same decision quotes IAS 21 para 16: "the essential feature of a non-monetary
  item is the absence of a right to receive… a fixed or determinable number of units of currency" [VERIFIED: same PDF]. If the holder
  can redeem 1 USDC for 1.00 USD, the holding looks **monetary**: it is retranslated at the closing rate and has FX gains and losses.
  If not (for example, only onboarded customers can redeem with the issuer), it is a **non-monetary** intangible held at historical
  cost. Different holders of the same token can classify it differently [ASSUMPTION: secondary, search summaries of KPMG
  "Stablecoins" IFRS insight and ICAEW Nov 2025, copied from stablecoin-accounting.md; not reopened, sites blocked].
- **2024–2026 standard-setters: nothing final.** In November 2025 the IASB put "cryptoassets" on a list of four topics it *may*
  start projects on as capacity allows. It is not on the work plan
  [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2025/december/iasb/ap8-work-plan-update.pdf].
  The FASB (US) added a research project in August 2025 on "whether certain payment digital assets are cash equivalents". It notes
  that ASU 2023-08 excludes assets that give "enforceable rights to… underlying… assets" (most fiat-backed stablecoins). It also notes
  the GENIUS Act became law on 18 July 2025
  [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2025/october/fasb-iasb-education-meeting/ap38-cryptocurrencies.pdf].
  The June 2026 FASB–IASB meeting had a FASB session "Crypto – Stablecoins and Transfers" and "Cash Equivalents—Disclosure
  Enhancement and Classification of Certain Digital Assets"
  [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2026/june/fasb-iasb-education-meeting/agenda.pdf].
  Big-4 views could not be opened (sites blocked).
- **Functional currency matters first.** Many crypto-native Singapore companies may have a **USD functional currency** under IAS 21.
  For them most of this FX problem goes away, leaving only a translation for presentation. The pain falls on companies whose
  functional currency really is SGD, INR or EUR [ASSUMPTION: my reasoning].
- **Singapore tax (IRAS), not opened:** payment tokens are treated as intangible property. Paying with them is a barter, so the
  recipient is taxed on the value of the goods or services. Whether a disposal gain is revenue or capital follows the usual badges
  of trade [ASSUMPTION: secondary, search summary of the IRAS e-Tax Guide "Income Tax Treatment of Digital Tokens" (2020)].
  **GST:** the 1 Jan 2020 digital-payment-token exemption *excludes tokens pegged to a currency*. Fiat-backed stablecoins may still
  be exempt under another paragraph (Fourth Schedule para 1(j)) [ASSUMPTION: secondary, search summaries of the IRAS GST DPT guide
  and Bloomberg Tax]. This is a question for a tax adviser, not for Clearline.
- **India / EU, one line each, not opened:** India taxes "virtual digital assets" at a flat rate with TDS on transfers, and
  receiving export proceeds in USDC raises FEMA questions [ASSUMPTION: not checked]. In the EU, MiCA makes USDC an e-money token
  with a claim on the issuer at par, which supports the financial-asset view [ASSUMPTION: secondary, not opened].

**Where it is genuinely unsettled:** classification (IAS 38 vs IFRS 9), and so whether month-end FX is booked at all, and in which
P&L line. The same economics give "impairment" in one company's books and "FX loss" in another's.

## 2. Where Xero fails, exactly

1. **No stablecoin currency.** Xero's `CurrencyCode` list is ISO-style codes. It has USD, SGD, INR and EUR, and **no USDC, USDT or
   any crypto code** [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/XeroAPI/Xero-OpenAPI/master/xero_accounting.yaml v19.1.0, `components.schemas.CurrencyCode`].
   Xero's currency product manager wrote (27 Feb 2023) that crypto needs "huge decimal precision, UX, currency rate feeds,
   revaluation logic, reporting" and "aren't prioritised"
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967121-users-setup/suggestions/44960431-multicurrency-support-for-new-currencies-bitco].
2. **Only bank accounts can hold a foreign currency.** Accounts API: `CurrencyCode` "For bank accounts only"
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://developer.xero.com/documentation/api/accounting/accounts].
   So the approved **Stablecoin clearing** account (non-bank current asset, decision 1) is **always in SGD**. Xero never knows it
   holds 3,000 USDC, only "SGD 4,310".
3. **Revaluation covers AR, AP and bank only.** The Foreign Currency Gains and Losses report "displays… three sections: accounts
   receivable, accounts payable, and bank accounts"
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://central.xero.com/s/article/Foreign-Currency-Gains-and-Losses-report].
   "Unrealised currency gains" covers "unpaid invoices or bills"; "Bank revaluation" covers "foreign currency bank accounts"
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://central.xero.com/s/article/Foreign-currency-accounts-in-the-chart-of-accounts-GL].
   **A non-bank asset account is never revalued, and a conversion out of it never produces a gain or loss.**
4. **Using a USD bank account as a proxy** gets revaluation, but against the policy (it presents the coin as cash). It also uses
   XE's USD/SGD mid-market rate, "the final rate at 11 pm" in the org time zone
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://central.xero.com/s/article/About-multicurrency], not the
   USDC/SGD rate actually traded. And it books cash FX as one unrealised "bank revaluation" figure, with no realised/unrealised
   split [ASSUMPTION: secondary, user post on Xero Product Ideas, https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967121-users-setup/suggestions/45766984-multicurrency-change-behaviour-of-unrealised-gai; confirm in the Demo Company].
5. **No lots or cost basis** for anything that is not an inventory item. Xero's own app store points users to Cryptio ("cost basis
   reports (FIFO and WAC)", "Export manual journals to Xero")
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://apps.xero.com/sg/app/cryptio].
6. **What Xero does get right:** a USD invoice paid into an SGD account is its "Scenario 1", and Xero books the realised FX on the
   receivable itself. `CurrencyRate` units are "[Foreign Currency] PER [Base Currency]" (USD per SGD), rounded to 6 dp; Xero says
   never default it to 1, and prefer `BankAmount`
   [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://developer.xero.com/documentation/best-practices/data-integrity/multicurrency].

**QuickBooks Online, short check.** QBO's help shows foreign-currency accounts created under "Bank and credit cards". "Income and
expense accounts always use your home currency". Rates come from IHS Markit, and users "adjust your home currency" by hand
[VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/multicurrency/learn-multicurrency-quickbooks-online/L5krkKQi8_US_en_US].
The SG help page says payments can only go to a home-currency bank account or one in the invoice's currency, and for anything else
suggests a "dummy bank account"
[VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-sg/help-article/multicurrency/receiving-making-payment-foreign-invoice-bill/L1weZLBmC_SG_en_SG].
I did not confirm whether a QBO Other Current Asset account can carry USD [ASSUMPTION]. QBO has no lots either.

## 3. Worked SGD example (DRAFT, for Aashmiin to check)

**Assumptions.** SGD functional and base currency. USDC = 1.00 USD every day (within the 0.5% band). No fees or spread. No GST
(an export service would be zero-rated; out of scope here). FIFO lots. Amounts rounded half-up to 0.01 SGD once, per line.

**Illustrative rates (made up, not market data):**

| Day | SGD per 1 USD | Xero `CurrencyRate` (USD per SGD, 6 dp) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.3500 | 0.740741 |
| 5 | 1.3400 | 0.746269 |
| 10 | 1.3300 | 0.751880 |
| 20 | 1.3200 | 0.757576 |
| 30 | 1.3100 | 0.763359 |

(Xero divides by its 6-dp rate: 5,000 / 0.751880 = 6,650.0027 → 6,650.00. Same cent here; on larger amounts the 6-dp rate can move
a cent. That is one reason Xero prefers `BankAmount`.)

### Correct entries

| Step | Account | Dr SGD | Cr SGD | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Day 1 buy 10,000 USDC | Stablecoin holding (CLR) | 13,500.00 | | Lot A: 10,000 @ 1.3500 |
| | Bank SGD | | 13,500.00 | |
| (b1) Day 5 invoice USD 5,000 | Accounts receivable | 6,700.00 | | 5,000 × 1.3400 |
| | Revenue | | 6,700.00 | |
| (b2) Day 10 receive 5,000 USDC | Stablecoin holding (CLR) | 6,650.00 | | Lot B: 5,000 @ 1.3300 |
| | Realised FX loss (on AR) | 50.00 | | 6,700.00 − 6,650.00 |
| | Accounts receivable | | 6,700.00 | |
| (c) Day 20 convert 12,000 USDC | Bank SGD | 15,840.00 | | 12,000 × 1.3200 |
| | Loss on conversion (FX loss under IFRS 9 view; disposal loss under IAS 38 view) | 320.00 | | |
| | Stablecoin holding (CLR) | | 16,160.00 | FIFO: Lot A 13,500.00 + 2,000 of Lot B @ 1.3300 = 2,660.00 |
| (d) Day 30, IFRS 9 / monetary view | Unrealised FX loss | 60.00 | | 3,000 × 1.3100 = 3,930.00 vs 3,990.00 |
| | Stablecoin holding (CLR) | | 60.00 | |
| (d) Day 30, IAS 38 cost view | Impairment loss | 60.00 | | Recoverable ≈ 3,930.00 < 3,990.00 |
| | Accumulated impairment | | 60.00 | Use **one** of the two (d) rows, not both |

**Lots after day 30:** Lot B remainder 3,000 USDC, cost SGD 3,990.00 (@ 1.3300), carrying amount 3,930.00.
**P&L for the month:** realised FX on AR −50.00, conversion −320.00, month-end −60.00, **total −430.00** (both views here, because
the rate fell). If day 30 were 1.3400 instead, the IFRS 9 view books a +30.00 gain and the IAS 38 cost view books nothing.
**FIFO vs average cost:** average is 20,150.00 / 15,000 = 1.343333 SGD per USDC. The conversion loss would then be 280.00 and the
month-end loss 100.00: the same 380.00 in total, split differently. The method has to be a written policy.

### What Xero does if you enter it the obvious way

**Way 1: the approved clearing account (non-bank, so SGD-only).**

| Step | Xero entry | Xero result | Correct | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Spend Money from Bank SGD 13,500.00, coded to CLR | CLR 13,500.00 | 13,500.00 | 0 |
| (b) | USD invoice; Payment USD 5,000 to CLR, no `CurrencyRate` | CLR +6,650.00, realised loss 50.00 (Xero books it) | same | 0 |
| (c) | Receive Money SGD 15,840.00 into Bank SGD, coded to CLR | CLR 20,150.00 − 15,840.00 = **4,310.00**, **no loss booked** | CLR 3,990.00, loss 320.00 | CLR overstated **320.00**; profit overstated 320.00 |
| (d) | Nothing: CLR has no currency, so it is not on the FX report | CLR **4,310.00** | 3,930.00 | CLR and profit overstated **380.00** |

**Way 2: a USD bank account named "USDC wallet" (common DIY, contrary to decision 1).** (a) is a transfer SGD 13,500 → USD 10,000,
and (b) is Xero's Scenario 2, with the same 50.00 realised loss. For (c), a transfer USD 12,000 → SGD 15,840. For (d), Xero values
the 3,000 USD balance at its day-30 rate: 3,930.00. **The total is right (−430.00), but the 380.00 on the coin shows as bank
revaluation (unrealised) rather than 320.00 realised + 60.00 unrealised. The coin is presented as cash. Rates are XE USD/SGD, not
USDC/SGD** [ASSUMPTION for the exact Xero split; test in the Demo Company].

**Clearest failure:** in the approved model, Xero holds the coin at a frozen SGD number. Each conversion silently leaves the
gain or loss inside the asset (320.00 here), and no Xero report will ever show it.

## 4. Who feels it, and how to test H2

- **Who:** the finance lead or outsourced accountant (Singapore corporate-service/accounting firms) of an SGD-, INR- or EUR-functional
  SME that is paid in USDC/USDT **and holds balances past month-end**: agencies, dev shops and B2B SaaS selling to crypto firms.
  [GUESS: no interviews yet.] Not affected: USD-functional companies, and anyone using a processor that settles to fiat (Stripe:
  stablecoin payments "settle in your Stripe balance in your local currency"
  [VERIFIED: accounting-integrations-engineer 2026-11-02, https://docs.stripe.com/payments/stablecoin-payments]).
- **How often:** every conversion, plus month-end and year-end audit.
- **What they do today:** a FIFO spreadsheet and manual journals [ASSUMPTION]; or enterprise tools:

| Integral | Not checked (site unreachable) | — | — |

- **Cost and who pays:** the company pays, through accountant hours or an enterprise subscription. The amounts are unknown [GUESS].
- **Why now:** GENIUS Act (July 2025), the FASB research project on cash equivalents, and cryptoassets on the IASB candidate list
  (all cited above). Auditors will ask for a written policy.
- **10 conversations would prove H2 if** ≥ 4 hold stablecoin balances across a month-end in a non-USD functional currency, **and** use
  Xero/QBO, **and** spend ≥ 2 hours a month on lots/FX or have had an audit adjustment, **and** ≥ 3 would pay ≥ US$100/month.
  **They would disprove it if** most convert the same day or settle to fiat, are USD-functional, or say their auditor accepts
  "immaterial, ignore". Ask: balance at last month-end; conversions per month; the policy the auditor accepted; how the
  spreadsheet works; the last adjustment.

## 5. Draft scores (research only, not a decision)

- **Pain 3:** real and exact (Xero hides the 320.00) for holders of balances, but the source is one advisor, not a customer.
- **Urgency 2:** it bites at audit and year-end, not daily; there is no regulatory deadline.
- **Willingness to pay 2:** enterprise tools already exist; SMEs with few conversions may stay on spreadsheets.
- **Reachability 3:** Singapore accounting firms are reachable for a solo founder; India is harder.
- **Regulatory risk 4 (5 = low):** read-only software. Classification and tax stay the accountant's call. Indian customers carry
  VDA/FEMA risk.
- **Fit with what's built 2:** Clearline values receipts at par, posts them to CLR and lets Xero book the FX on the receivable.
  Lots, conversions and month-end revaluation are not built. Xero cannot hold the coin quantity, so this would be a new
  sub-ledger product.
