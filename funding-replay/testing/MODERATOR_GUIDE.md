# Moderator guide: 15 minutes, problem first

**Goal:** learn how this person actually decides when to fund an overseas supplier payment, then watch them use
the tester edition without help. **Not the goal:** pitching or validating the demo. Talk 20%, listen 80%.
Write their words, not your summary. Don't mention savings, stablecoins, AI or what you hope they'll say.

**Before the call:** open the tester (link or the unzipped `index.html`) on the **Friday funding decision**,
**Reference week**, rate **8%**. Have the interview log open (`interview_log.csv`), and ask permission to take
notes.

## 0:00–1:00 · Set up
"Thanks for the time. I'm trying to understand how teams decide when to fund supplier payments abroad. There are
no right answers, and I'm not selling anything today. Is it OK if I take notes?"

## 1:00–7:00 · The last real payment (no screen)
Open with:
> **"Walk me through the last overseas supplier payment where you had to put cash aside earlier than you wanted."**

If they've never had that:
> **"Walk me through your most recent overseas supplier payment. Who chose the funding date, and why?"**

Let them tell it. Use these only to fill gaps, in your own words, one at a time:
- What was due, to whom, and by when? (amount range, currency, corridor, time zone)
- Who picked the funding date? Who signed it off?
- Why that date: the bank or provider required it, an internal policy did, or the team wasn't sure and
  played safe?
- What did you actually know at that point? (balance, quote, cutoff, incoming receipts) Where did it come from?
- If you'd kept the cash a few days longer, what would it have done? (repaid a facility, earned interest,
  nothing)
- Would a spreadsheet, a calendar reminder, different contract terms or your existing credit line already handle
  this?
- How often does this happen? Has it ever cost money, a late fee, or a supplier relationship?

Probe with: "What happened next?" · "Why?" · "Can you show me roughly what that looked like?" Avoid "Would you…?"
questions about the future.

## 7:00–13:00 · Three tasks, no coaching
Say once: "Please think aloud. If something is unclear, that's useful. I won't explain, but I'll note it."

- **Task A (Friday).** Reference week, Friday funding decision. Ask: **"What decision would you make, and which
  evidence led you there?"** Write their answer verbatim *before* anything else.
- **Task B (hindsight).** "Now switch to Retrospective comparison, then back to Friday." Ask: **"What did you
  learn later that you could not have known on Friday?"**
- **Task C (information vs cash).** "Please open *Friday-afternoon balance* at the Monday checkpoint, then
  *Forecast customer receipt* at the Monday checkpoint." Ask: **"In which case do you need better information,
  and in which case are you actually short of cash?"**

For each task, note: **what they clicked**, **what they misread**, **whether it changed their decision**, and
**what they'd use instead** (spreadsheet, bank portal, provider email). Mark each note **U** (usability: the demo
confused them) or **B** (business: evidence about their real process). Understanding the demo is not evidence
they'd buy it.

## 13:00–15:00 · Close
- "If we replayed one real week of your payments, with names and account numbers removed, who could approve
  sharing that?" (Record yes / no / who.)
- "Who owns the budget for tools like this?" Only if they raised a cost themselves: "If a four-week pilot on
  one corridor cost SGD 500, who would decide, and what would it need to show?"
- "Who else should I talk to?" · Thank them. Don't promise features.

## After the call (5 minutes, same day)
Fill one row of `interview_log.csv`. Quote, don't paraphrase. Mark anything inferred as such.
